Design brief · Forerunner 265 · Connect IQ

Trinity

A watch face for people who run a full work day, train once or twice a day, and still want a life outside both. Three arcs show where the day stands on WORK, BODY and LIFE. A readiness score at the centre, built from sleep, Body Battery and stress, raises or lowers the targets.

Work: the day's load Body: output vs target Life: recharge time

The face is the first piece. A calendar bridge and an on-watch app come after it, as laid out below.

Research

How executive athletes actually run their days

The design rests on a small body of evidence and a lot of consistent public reporting. The pattern holds across very different leaders: training is booked like a meeting, recovery is planned rather than left to chance, and the calendar is where the trade-offs get made.

Energy, not time, is the constraint

Loehr and Schwartz's "corporate athlete" work argues that sustained high performance comes from cycling between stress and recovery, the way athletes train. That cycle is the whole idea behind Trinity: no pillar should stay maxed out while another sits empty.

Loehr & Schwartz, "The Making of a Corporate Athlete," Harvard Business Review, 2001.

Fit CEOs, stronger firms

A study of S&P 1500 CEOs from 2001 to 2011 found that firms led by CEOs who finished a marathon were valued about 5% higher, after controls. This is a correlation and not proof of cause, but boards and investors cite it.

"Does CEO Fitness Matter?" (Limbach & Sonnenburg), reported by Canadian Running.

Sleep is the multiplier

The adult consensus is seven or more hours. Executives who train hard on short sleep do worse at both. That is why Trinity lowers the body target when readiness is low instead of pushing harder.

AASM / Sleep Research Society consensus statement, 2015.

Publicly reported routines

  • Bob Iger wrote that he gets up around 4:15 a.m. to exercise and think before the work day begins.
  • Tim Cook is reported to start before 5 a.m. with email and then the gym.
  • Anna Wintour is known for an early tennis session every day.
  • Richard Branson credits daily exercise (kitesurfing, tennis, cycling) with roughly doubling his productivity.
  • Barack Obama kept a daily workout of about 45 minutes through his presidency.
  • Warren Buffett and Bill Gates both treat reading as protected time: Buffett spends most of his day reading, Gates about 50 books a year. Reading is part of Trinity for that reason.

What the pattern means for the design

  • The morning block is sacred. Most training happens before 8 a.m. The face needs to show output early and not nag during meetings.
  • The failure mode is stacking. A hard session, a short night and a high-stress board day each look fine on their own. Together they add up to overreach. Readiness has to modulate the targets.
  • The calendar is the control surface. These people already block "Gym" and "Read" in the calendar. Trinity should read those blocks and never ask for manual logging on the wrist.
  • Glanceable over detailed. A two-second look between meetings. One nudge, never a dashboard.
  • Discretion. A watch face is visible across a boardroom table. Show "MTG IN 8m", never the meeting title.

Capabilities

What the Forerunner 265 will give a third-party face

Garmin exposes a lot through the Complications API (Connect IQ 4.2 and later), but not everything you see in Garmin's own widgets. These limits shaped the design, so they matter more than anything else in this brief.

Time, date, heart rate, steps and step goalYesFace basics. Steps are 25% of BODY.
Calories today (total)YesActive calories are estimated as total minus resting burn from your profile (Mifflin-St Jeor), prorated to the time of day.
Active calories as Garmin shows themEstimatedNo direct API. The estimate should land close to Garmin's figure; check it against Garmin Connect for a week.
Intensity minutes (day and week, weekly goal)Yes15% of BODY, against one-seventh of your weekly goal.
Body Battery, stress levelYesCore inputs to READY. Stress history also gives "calm minutes" for LIFE.
Recovery time (hours remaining)YesSubtracts up to 25 points from READY.

Bed and wake timesYesSets when calm time starts counting and when "WIND DOWN" appears.
HRV status, Training Readiness scoreNoNot exposed to third-party faces. READY is Trinity's own stand-in.
Reading timeNo sensorComes from "Read" blocks in your calendar through the Bridge, and later from a reading timer in the Trinity app.

AMOLED always-on mode limits how many pixels may stay lit and requires burn-in protection. In that mode Trinity drops the arcs and shows only a dimmed time and the next event, shifted a few pixels each minute.

The model

How each arc fills

PillarFormulaWhy
READY (core)0.45·BodyBattery + 0.30·SleepScore + 0.25·(100 − Stress), minus up to 25 for recovery hours (72 h max)Weights shift automatically to the signals that exist. Green at 70 or above, amber from 40, red below 40.
BODY0.60·active kcal ÷ adjusted goal + 0.25·steps ÷ goal + 0.15·intensity min ÷ (weekly goal ÷ 7)The adjusted goal is 100% of your target at READY 70+, 80% from 40 to 69, and 60% below 40. A bad night gives you a smaller target.
WORKprogress through the work window (default 08:00–17:00, Mon–Fri)The arc turns amber after more than an hour of overtime, or when meetings exceed your daily cap (default 4 h, needs the Bridge).
LIFEcalm minutes since waking ÷ goal. With the Bridge: 50% calm + 50% (personal + reading min) ÷ (goals)Calm minutes are time at Garmin's "rest" stress level while awake: a measured proxy for actually switching off.

Nudges, in priority order

The face shows one line at the bottom. The first rule that matches wins. When an event starts within 10 minutes, the next-event line above it turns white instead.

  • RECOVERY DAY: READY is under 30.
  • BREATHE 2 MIN: stress is 76 or higher.
  • WRAP UP WORK: more than an hour past the end of the work day.
  • GUARD A GAP: meetings are over the cap and the work day is not over.
  • TRAIN WINDOW: after 15:00, BODY is under half, READY is 50 or higher, and bedtime is more than 2.5 h away.
  • READ 20 MIN: after 19:00 and reading is short of the goal.
  • WIND DOWN: within an hour of your bedtime.
  • WALK 10: after 11:00, under 30% of the step goal.
  • IN BALANCE: BODY 90%+ and LIFE 60%+.
Calendar and privacy

The Trinity Bridge

The native calendar complication only gives a start time. To tell a meeting from lunch from a reading block, Trinity uses a small script that runs in your own calendar account. It reads today's events, sorts them into categories by keywords in the title, and returns only minutes per category plus the next category. Titles, attendees and locations never leave your account.

Default keywords: TRAIN for gym, run, ride, swim, workout, yoga, tennis or golf; READ for read or book; LUNCH; PERS for family, kids, dinner or doctor; FOCUS for focus, deep work or hold. Events with other guests that match no keyword count as MTG.

The Google Calendar version is written and tested. If your work calendar is Outlook / Microsoft 365, the same logic needs a Microsoft Graph version (a Power Automate flow or a small Azure Function). That depends on how Vistek IT allows calendar access.

Roadmap

Build order

  1. Phase 1 · code drafted

    Trinity watch face

    The three arcs, READY, the stats row, the next event and nudges, using on-watch data only. Settings in Garmin Connect for the calorie goal, work hours, meeting cap and the calm, personal and reading goals. Needs a first build in the Connect IQ simulator.

  2. Phase 2 · Google version drafted

    Trinity Bridge

    Calendar categories, meeting load and reading blocks. Google Apps Script is written and its logic tests pass. A Microsoft 365 variant is pending your calendar decision.

  3. Phase 3 · next

    Trinity app and glance

    An on-watch app for what a face cannot do: a full breakdown screen for each pillar, a "Start reading" timer, a two-minute breathing reset when stress spikes, and a weekly balance view showing which pillar went short. The glance shows today's READY and the weakest pillar.

  4. Phase 4

    Weekly review

    A Sunday summary on the phone or web: balance by day, nights under seven hours, overtime hours and reading minutes. This is the executive view, and where the targets for the week ahead get adjusted.

Decisions needed

Before the first build

  • Calendar source: Google, Outlook / Microsoft 365, or both. This decides which Bridge to finish.
  • Targets: the daily active-calorie goal (the draft default is 900 kcal, which suits one to two hours of training), work hours, and the daily meeting cap.
  • Distribution: sideload onto your own watch only, or publish to the Connect IQ Store, which needs a developer account, a review and a privacy statement.
  • Developer setup: a Garmin developer account and the Connect IQ SDK with the VS Code extension, to compile and run the face in the simulator.
